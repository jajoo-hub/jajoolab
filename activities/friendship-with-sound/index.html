Activity 04

Friendship with Sound

Notice when a voice became louder, then talk about it later when calm.

Introduction

Friendship with Sound is a simple family activity that helps children notice and communicate moments when their voice became louder.

Sometimes, when we experience different feelings or situations, we may shout without really noticing it in the moment.

The activity gives children an opportunity to notice when they shouted, remember what happened, and share the moment with a parent later, when it works for their family.

There is no right or wrong answer. The purpose is simply to notice, remember, and communicate, not to correct the child or identify a particular emotion.

Before you begin

Please let your child notice these moments naturally during everyday life.

You do not need to ask your child immediately after they have shouted.

Instead, when your child is calm and both of you have time, you can ask whether they remember a moment when their voice became louder and invite them to share what happened.

If your child does not remember a moment or does not want to talk about it, that is completely okay.

This activity is not intended to interrupt or manage an intense moment when it is happening. Please handle such moments in whatever way is appropriate for your family.

How to do it

When your child notices that they shouted, they can remember the moment and tell you about it later.

When you have a suitable moment together, you can simply ask:

“Do you remember a time when you shouted?”

Give your child space to describe what happened in their own words.

You might listen for:

  • What was happening?
  • What did they say or do?
  • What happened just before they shouted?
  • What happened afterwards?

Your child does not need to identify an emotion or give a reason if they don't know one.

You can share your own experiences too. The focus is on listening and understanding the child's experience, rather than correcting their behaviour.

Most importantly

The parent’s role is simply to listen.

There is no need to judge, label, or fix the moment.

The goal is to help the child gradually become more aware of their own experiences and feel comfortable communicating them with a parent.
