Body Weather Check

A simple weather-word check-in for noticing how someone feels in an ordinary moment.

Pocket Recall · Hold this in mind for the week
“My weather is cloudy today because I’m a bit tired. What’s your body weather right now?”

When to try

At a random, normal time during the day (never during an active tantrum or crisis).

The parent’s role

Share your own weather first. Accept their answer without attempting to fix or cheer them up.

The child’s role

Can say sunny, cloudy, stormy, or invent their own weather word. No explanation required.

The problem we are looking at

Asking children “How are you feeling?” frequently meets a one-word wall: “Fine” or “Good.”

Emotional vocabulary like “anxious,” “frustrated,” or “overwhelmed” can feel heavy, analytical, and intimidating for young minds.

Weather words are naturally neutral and free of blame. Nobody feels guilty for a rainy day. By using weather, children can notice their internal state without feeling judged or defective.

The three core weather words

☀️

Sunny

Warm, light, energized, calm, or content.

☁️

Cloudy

Tired, quiet, daydreaming, or a bit grey.

⛈️

Stormy

Turbulent, restless, heavy, or windy.

✕ What NOT to do (Crucial Guardrails)

  • ✕
    Never try to "fix" cloudy or stormy weather. If your child says “I’m stormy,” resist saying “Oh no! What can we do to make you sunny?” Simply accept: “Stormy. Thank you for telling me.”
  • ✕
    Never use this immediately after a heated meltdown or fight. Avoid asking right after they’ve become very angry or upset. Handle that moment in your family’s usual way, and save this practice for calm, ordinary moments.
  • ✕
    Never demand an explanation or reason. If they don’t want to explain why they are cloudy, that is completely fine. The practice is noticing, not psychoanalyzing.
  • ✕
    Never attach moral judgment to words. Sunny is not “good” and stormy is not “bad.” All weather is natural, normal, and passes.

The Golden Principle: Weather Changes On Its Own

Just like rain clouds outside, feelings pass naturally when acknowledged without resistance. The goal is to notice what is present, not to manufacture sunshine.

How to do it in 4 steps

1

Pick an Ordinary Moment

Choose a normal, neutral time — sitting on the couch, walking to school, or eating a snack.

2

Share Your Weather First

Start with yourself: “My weather is cloudy today because I’m feeling a little tired.”

3

Ask Without Pressure

Ask: “What’s your body weather right now?” Let them pick any word or weather metaphor they like.

4

Acknowledge and Continue

Respond with warmth: “Got it, thank you for sharing that with me.” Carry on with your day.

Parent Field Notes

What to notice this week

After trying this once or twice, take 30 seconds to reflect:

  • 01 Did your child answer quickly, or did they hesitate to find a word?
  • 02 Did you notice any difference between what they said and how their body appeared?
  • 03 Did you share your own weather without feeling the need to explain it away?
  • 04 Did the check-in feel easy, awkward, or surprisingly ordinary?
